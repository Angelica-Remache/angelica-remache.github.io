## Working Papers

**Exit over Voice: Corruption as a Migration Push Factor**
<br>*Presented at:* The Migration Conference 2026 (Bratislava); Berkeley Latin American Politics Workshop (2026); Latin American Studies Association (2025).

<details class="abstract-toggle">
<summary>Abstract</summary>
<p>What drives emigration when wages are rising and poverty is falling? This paper argues that petty corruption, the everyday experience of paying bribes to access public services and opportunities, functions as a distinct push factor for emigration, operating independently from the economic hardship and violence that the existing literature emphasizes. Using eight waves of LAPOP AmericasBarometer data (2006&ndash;2023) across 17 Latin American countries, I employ entropy balancing to examine the relationship between corruption and migration. I find that individuals who report experiencing petty corruption are 6.4 percentage points more likely to report emigration intentions than otherwise similar individuals. Evidence from 52 semi-structured interviews with Ecuadorian citizens and immigrants in the U.S. reveals two mechanisms: (1) a material channel, where bribes gate-keep access to services and opportunities, and (2) an institutional trust channel, where impunity signals that change is impossible. Corruption reframes the choice for many: exit over voice.</p>
